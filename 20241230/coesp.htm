Emergency Readiness

Emergency Safety Procedures

Earthquake, Fire, and Terrorist Attack procedures for classroom, laboratory, campus, and field-training contexts.

Stay Alert

Recognize danger early, listen for instructions, and avoid panic-driven movement.

Act Fast

Use the safest available action based on the actual threat, location, and exit access.

Report Clearly

Give concise information to authorities: what happened, where it happened, and who needs help.

Before an Earthquake

  • Know the evacuation routes, open-area assembly points, and emergency exits of the room, building, or laboratory.
  • Identify safe cover points such as sturdy tables, desks, or interior walls away from glass, shelves, and unsecured objects.
  • Keep aisles, exits, and laboratory pathways clear at all times.
  • Secure heavy equipment, cabinets, chemicals, glassware, and materials that may fall or spill during shaking.
  • Prepare a small emergency kit with water, flashlight, whistle, basic first aid supplies, identification, and essential medication.

During an Earthquake

  • Drop to your hands and knees immediately.
  • Cover your head and neck. Take cover under a sturdy table or desk when available.
  • Hold on until the shaking stops. If the cover moves, move with it while staying protected.
  • Stay away from windows, glass panels, shelves, cabinets, hanging objects, and unstable equipment.
  • Do not run outside while the ground is shaking.
  • If outdoors, move to an open area away from buildings, trees, utility poles, walls, and power lines.

After an Earthquake

  • Check yourself and nearby classmates for injuries before moving.
  • Evacuate calmly when instructed or when it is safe to do so.
  • Use stairs. Do not use elevators.
  • Proceed to the designated assembly area and wait for roll call or accountability checks.
  • Expect aftershocks and be ready to Drop, Cover, and Hold On again.
  • Report injuries, trapped persons, fire, gas smell, chemical spills, electrical hazards, and structural damage immediately.

Before a Fire

  • Know at least two exit routes from the room, laboratory, building, or activity area.
  • Know the location of fire alarms, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and assembly areas.
  • Keep exits, corridors, stairways, and laboratory pathways clear.
  • Do not overload outlets or misuse electrical equipment.
  • Handle flammable materials, chemicals, burners, and electrical devices only as instructed.

During a Fire

  • Alert others immediately and activate the alarm if safe and appropriate.
  • Evacuate using the nearest safe exit. Do not collect unnecessary belongings.
  • Stay low when moving through smoke because cleaner air is usually closer to the floor.
  • Before opening a door, check for heat using the back of your hand. Do not open a hot door.
  • Close doors behind you to slow the spread of fire and smoke.
  • Use stairs. Do not use elevators.
  • If clothing catches fire, stop, drop, and roll.

After a Fire

  • Proceed to the designated assembly area and wait for accountability checks.
  • Do not re-enter the building until authorized by emergency personnel or school authorities.
  • Report missing persons, injuries, trapped individuals, smoke exposure, and possible hazards.
  • Avoid touching burned materials, damaged electrical equipment, chemicals, debris, or structural components.
  • Cooperate with emergency responders and provide accurate information when asked.

Before a Terrorist Attack

  • Maintain situational awareness in classrooms, laboratories, hallways, parking areas, public spaces, and event venues.
  • Know exits, evacuation routes, shelter areas, lockdown procedures, and emergency contact channels.
  • Report suspicious behavior, unattended bags, unusual packages, threats, or security concerns to authorities immediately.
  • Do not touch, move, open, or inspect suspicious objects.
  • Keep your phone charged and know how to send your location during emergencies.

During a Terrorist Attack

  • If escape is safe, run away from the threat and move toward a secure location.
  • Leave belongings behind. Help others only when doing so does not place you in greater danger.
  • If escape is not possible, hide in a secure area, lock or barricade doors, silence devices, stay low, and remain quiet.
  • Keep away from windows, open spaces, and visible lines of sight.
  • If there is an explosion, drop to the ground, protect your head and neck, and move away from secondary hazards when safe.
  • If exposed to smoke, dust, or chemical irritants, cover your nose and mouth with available cloth and move away from the affected area if possible.
  • As a last resort and only when your life is in immediate danger, fight using decisive action to disrupt the attacker and create an opportunity to escape.

After a Terrorist Attack

  • Keep your hands visible when approaching or being approached by responding officers.
  • Follow commands from law enforcement and emergency responders immediately.
  • Do not make sudden movements, point, shout unnecessarily, or hold objects that may be mistaken for weapons.
  • Move away from the danger area and avoid crowds where secondary threats may occur.
  • Report injuries, attacker description, suspicious objects, possible explosives, fire, smoke, or trapped persons.
  • Do not spread unverified information. Wait for official instructions before leaving the assembly or holding area.

Preparation for Crisis

  • Always be prepared. Know the emergency plan before an incident occurs.
  • Exercise regularly and maintain physical readiness because evacuation, rescue movement, and emergency response require stamina.
  • Practice calm decision-making under pressure. Panic reduces observation, judgment, and survival action.
  • Memorize key contact numbers and know where to report emergencies.
  • Keep essential items accessible: identification, whistle, flashlight, water, basic first aid, power bank, and medication if needed.
  • For criminology and forensic science students, preserve safety first. Documentation, observation, and evidence awareness come only after immediate life safety is secured.
  • Follow the chain of command, cooperate with authorities, and avoid interfering with emergency operations.

These procedures complement institutional emergency policies and should be adjusted according to official school protocols, local disaster-risk reduction guidance, and instructions from emergency responders.
